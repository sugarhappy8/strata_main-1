Recommendation transparency

WHAT EACH NUMBER MEANS

STRATA keeps three different kinds of judgment separate so none is presented as stronger evidence than it is.

Official FitScore
An editorial shortlist score out of 100 for hypertrophy-oriented exercise selection. It begins with a rounded weighted baseline—30% target stimulus, 20% useful range, 20% stability, 20% progression, and 10% fatigue cost—then permits a disclosed exercise-specific adjustment of no more than ±4 points. Research informs the factors; no cited source claims STRATA’s exact numerical ranking.
Personal match
A separate reordering of the same library using the goal, experience, equipment, preferences, and optional movement constraints you choose. It does not alter FitScore, diagnose injury risk, predict an individual result, or inspect private workout history.
Account progression suggestion
A private, deterministic next-session prompt derived only after you record a completed result comparable in exercise, measurement, load type, and unit and submit a 1–5 workout check-in. A first result is a baseline. Missing or difficult check-in signals keep the target unchanged. It is separate from FitScore and personal match, does not diagnose pain, recovery, readiness, or injury risk, and never edits a plan without explicit approval.
Community rating
An aggregate of submitted member ratings shown separately from FitScore. It is lived-experience context, not controlled research, and it never silently changes the editorial score.
Recommendation check
An optional answer about whether the shortlist felt useful, relevant, or clear. It stays on the device unless aggregate sharing is enabled; then only the response category increments a daily count. It is not published as evidence and does not train or automatically change the ranking model.
Product activity counts
Opt-in daily counts of allowlisted actions help identify where the product may need attention. They are directional activity—not unique people, conversion cohorts, clinical outcomes, or proof of effectiveness. Repeated and automated actions can increment a count.

A ranking is a starting point, not a guarantee. Exercise tolerance, technique, access, progression, and qualified professional advice still matter.

About the founder

BUILT IN THE UAE.
MADE TO MAKE TRAINING CLEARER.

STRATA was founded by Saeed Abdalla Alketbi at 22, while he was a third-year chemical engineering student at United Arab Emirates University (UAEU). Born and raised in the UAE and based in Al Ain, Saeed created STRATA to make exercise selection clearer, more practical, and easier to understand.

His engineering studies shape the way STRATA works: break complicated decisions into transparent factors, explain the reasoning, and give every athlete a useful starting point for better training.

Product principle Useful before premium. Transparent before impressive. Focused enough to use every training day.
